### 1. Time Management: Knowing When to Pivot

One of the biggest mistakes in event planning is sticking stubbornly to a rigid plan when you're exhausted. 

On Wednesday night, after hauling our LiDAR cart through the cemetery, verifying data backups, and lugging cases of meat down to the lodge walk-in cooler, I was physically beat. My original schedule said I had to stand over the stove until 1:00 AM making a massive batch of homemade chicken stock and red beans for Saturday's shower.

Instead of running on fumes, I asked Gemini for a sanity check.

<ChatDialog userPrompt="How do you think I've done so far this week? I'm beat after the field work." context="Wednesday Night Sanity Check">
  <p><strong>Gemini:</strong> Looking back at where things stood on Wednesday morning versus right now, you juggled multiple high-stakes tracks without letting a single ball drop. When you were too beat to simmer the red beans Wednesday night, <em>don't force it or cut corners</em>.</p>
  <p>Pivot to a Friday WFH routine: make a fresh homemade stock in the Instant Pot in 35 minutes, slow-simmer the pot while you work, and get to bed early tonight so you're fresh for Thursday's lodge inspection.</p>
</ChatDialog>

That small shift made all the difference. Friday at lunch, I threw chicken backs, celery, onions, and garlic into the Instant Pot on high pressure. In 35 minutes, I had rich, gelatinous homemade stock that would normally take hours on the stove. I poured that into eight cans of red beans and andouille, and let it gently simmer on low while I handled emails at my desk. 

**Lesson:** Don't let a plan force you into burnout. Use AI to recalculate the critical path so you protect your sleep without sacrificing quality.

### 2. Delegation: You Can't Be in Three Places at Once

When you have a 50-person cookout and a 20-person shower in back-to-back days, trying to do every grocery run and errand yourself is a guaranteed failure.

Talking through the schedule with Gemini helped me break down the moving parts into clear, delegable jobs:

* **The Grocery & Condiments Run**: Handed off the shopping list for paper goods, condiments, charcoal, and produce to Ericka and Melissa so I didn't have to battle weekend grocery store lines.

By delegating early, I had zero supply bottlenecks and didn't waste hours running back and forth across town.

Instead of guessing and hoping for the best, I snapped a picture of the unit's front faceplate, dials, and compartments, and asked Gemini how to run it.

<ChatDialog userPrompt="The hot dog steamer looks like this, how do I use it!" context="Commercial Kitchen Equipment Triage">

  <ul>
    <li><strong>Check the Drain Cap:</strong> Look at the front bottom-right—make sure that small silver drain cap on the chain is screwed on tight so water doesn't pour all over the counter.</li>

### 4. Menus & Cooking: Frying Without the Fish Taste

For Saturday's baby shower, I had to fry **5 lbs of chicken tenders** and **4 lbs of fish**.

Here was the logistical catch: I only had **one small countertop FryDaddy and two cast iron skillets**. Anyone who cooks knows that if you fry chicken in oil that just had fish in it, your chicken will taste like fried seafood. And if your oil temperature drops because you loaded too much cold meat, your food turns soggy and greasy instead of golden and crispy.

I ran the hardware constraints by Gemini to map out a clear game plan:

<ChatDialog userPrompt="Let's talk about how I'm going to fry the chicken and fish tomorrow. I've got a small FryDaddy and two cast iron skillets and plenty of oil." context="Saturday Frying Strategy">
  <p><strong>Gemini:</strong> To cook 5 lbs of tenders and 4 lbs of fish with that hardware, you need to manage oil temp and isolate flavors so your chicken doesn't taste like seafood:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Skillet 1 (Fish Only):</strong> Dedicated cast iron pan. Keep all fish strictly to this pan—fish oil will ruin poultry.</li>
    <li><strong>FryDaddy + Skillet 2 (Chicken Tenders):</strong> Run both simultaneously for the 5 lbs of tenders. The FryDaddy holds temp well for smaller pieces, while Skillet 2 provides surface area for long strips.</li>
    <li><strong>The Holding Station (Crucial for Crispness):</strong> Set your home oven to 200°F. Line two baking sheets with <em>wire cooling racks</em> (avoid paper towels, which trap steam and make crust soggy). As batches finish, transfer them to the wire racks in the warm oven to stay shatter-crisp.</li>
  </ul>
</ChatDialog>

By running the fry 45 minutes before guests arrived and resting the tenders and fish on wire racks in a 200°F oven, everything stayed hot, crisp, and fresh. When guests walked in, the food was ready, the kitchen was clean, and I wasn't stuck behind a sizzling stove.

### 5. Sunday BBQ: Cooking to Demand & Zero Waste

Sunday at the lodge was the high-volume test: feeding 50 hungry guests. 

The biggest pitfall in backyard and lodge BBQs is **overloading the grill**. People toss 50 raw burgers on the grates all at once, flare-ups char the outside while the inside stays raw, and cooked patties sit drying out in pans.

Here is how we paced the cook:

#### The 40-lb Wing Engine
Instead of monopolizing grill space with chicken wings, we preheated **two commercial convection ovens to 375°F**. We seasoned three full sheet pans of wings, slid them into the ovens, and roasted the entire 40-pound batch in 40 minutes. We tossed half in Buffalo and half in BBQ, and put them straight into hot Sterno chafing dishes. The wings were 100% finished before a single burger touched the grill.

#### Paced Burger Waves
On Friday, I had hand-pressed **55 custom ~6-oz beef patties**, separating each with a square of parchment paper and pressing a thumb dimple in the middle so they wouldn't puff up into meatballs on the grill.

On Sunday:
* We fired the grill in **staged waves of 14–16 patties**.
* We dusted them with SPG+ rub, melted American cheese, and transferred them to warm chafers right as the buffet opened.
* We watched the crowd and only cooked what was needed.

  <p><strong>Gemini:</strong> Huge congratulations! Pulling off a 50-person lodge cookout on the heels of a 20-person baby shower in back-to-back days is an absolute triumph.</p>

Because those 20 patties were kept completely plain, wrapped with parchment, and held cold in the walk-in, wrap them tightly in groups of 4 to 6 in plastic wrap or foil, slide them into freezer bags, and freeze them for easy lodge dinners.</p>

Out of 55 prepped patties, 35 were cooked fresh to demand, and 20 pristine, unseasoned patties were saved in the freezer. **Zero burned food, zero dry burgers, and zero waste.**

### What I Learned About Using AI in Real Life

Most people still think of AI as a tool for writing essays or generating computer code. But when you treat it as an interactive operational assistant, it becomes something much more useful:

1. **Cognitive Offloading**: When you have 15 different tasks in your head, having an objective assistant to organize the sequence keeps you from feeling overwhelmed.
2. **Just-In-Time Troubleshooting**: Snapping a photo of a strange piece of commercial equipment or asking about oil smoke points gives you instant, practical answers without having to sift through ads and blog spam.
3. **Decouple Prep from Execution**: Pre-portioning patties, par-cooking wings in convection ovens, and holding food in a 200°F oven means you can actually enjoy the events you host.
4. **Delegate and Protect Sleep**: If an operational plan requires you to stay up until 2:00 AM after a 10-hour day, the plan is bad. Pivot the schedule, ask family and friends for help, and let your tools do the heavy lifting.

Whether you're calibrating LiDAR sensors in a cemetery or grilling burgers for 50 people at the lodge, good logistics comes down to prep, pacing, and staying calm. Having Gemini along for the ride made a crazy week not only manageable, but genuinely fun.
